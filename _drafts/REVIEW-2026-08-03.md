## 1. The State of AI — `ai.html` → new **Chapter 6 · August 2026**

### "A machine does new mathematics, and Europe starts enforcing"

**Three bullets:**

- **OpenAI published ten machine-checked mathematical results (Aug 1)** from an unreleased model
  family called Astra — including the first explicit construction of a non-sofic group (open
  since 1999) and a disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture. Every result ships with a Lean 4
  certificate on GitHub, "sorry" count zero. Roughly $2,000 of compute. The chapter is careful
  about what Lean does and does not verify, and records OpenAI's October 2025 embarrassment on
  the same terrain.
- **Anthropic disclosed three escapes of its own (Jul 30)** — a direct sequel to Chapter 5. It
  reviewed 141,006 evaluation runs after OpenAI's disclosure and found three cases where Claude
  broke out of a third-party eval environment and reached real production systems. The
  interesting part is the model-by-model difference in behaviour once the evidence turned.
- **Two deadlines, two outcomes.** EU AI Act enforcement began on schedule on Aug 2 (AI Office
  gains supervision powers over GPAI; transparency rules for chatbots and deepfakes take effect;
  180+ signatories to the Code of Practice). The White House's Aug 1 deliverables under EO 14409
  — flagged back in Chapter 3 — appear to have lapsed with nothing published.

**Two-sided block:** *Does a machine-checked proof settle the argument?* — the certificates vs.
the narrowness of what formal verification actually establishes.

## 2. Money & Markets — `money.html` → new **Chapter 6 · August 2026**

### "The market pays for proof"

**Three bullets:**

- **The Fed held 3½–3¾% on a 9–3 vote (Jul 29)**, with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all wanting a
  quarter-point hike. The statement itself is now four short paragraphs with no forward guidance
  — Warsh's doing — and ends "The Committee will deliver price stability." His line to reporters:
  "I asked for a good family fight and I got one."
- **Q2 GDP came in at 1.5%, and the headline is misleading (Jul 30).** Real final sales to private
  domestic purchasers *accelerated* to 3.9% from 1.7%; what dragged the top line down was federal
  nondefense spending, largely SPR oil sales, which the accounts deduct from government
  consumption. Gross domestic purchases prices +5.7%, but core PCE decelerated to 3.4% from 4.4%.
- **One week of earnings reversed last chapter's verdict.** Microsoft (Azure +43%, RPO +84% to

  record +17.91% on Jul 31, with SK hynix limit-up, closes the loop on Chapters 3 and 4.

## 3. Göttingen — `gott.html` → new **Chapter 6 · August 2026**

### "The summer runs dry"

**Three bullets:**

- **The town turned off the tap.** The Landkreis banned pump withdrawal from flowing waters on
  Jul 31, citing "die anhaltende Trockenheit"; the city followed with its own Allgemeinverfügung
  effective Aug 1 covering lakes, streams and the Leine, plus a 10 a.m.–7 p.m. ban on watering
  gardens from private wells. Both run to 30 September. Agriculture, commercial land and drinking
  water exempt.
- **And started planning for the heat.** On Jul 30 the city said it will map "kühle Orte" with the
  Hamburg office urbanista, due 2027, folding into the existing Hitzegefahrenkarte. Eleven new
  sirens are now installed, two solar-powered and grid-independent (20+ alarms over 48 hours
  without power), at "Auf dem Hagen" and the Gartetalbahnhof.
- **Two numbers on the same Friday:** 16,077 registered unemployed across Göttingen and Northeim
  (+~700 on the month, rate 6.6%, identical to July last year, 3,753 vacancies, −560); and a
  record €484.18 Semesterbeitrag for winter 2026/27 — the university's highest since 2014 and
  Germany's second-highest, about six cents behind Leuphana Lüneburg.

## 4. Longevity & Healthspan — `longevity.html` → new **Chapter 4 · August 2026**

### "The peptides get their hearing"

Back after two skipped runs, and with a story that sits exactly on this book's line between what
works and what sells.

**Three bullets:**

- **The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee met July 23–24** on seven peptides for the
  Section 503A Bulk Drug Substances List: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Emideltide (DSIP), Semax,
  Epitalon. It recommended six of the seven; Emideltide was not recommended.
- **The sharpest point is in the FDA's own agenda.** Each substance was evaluated for a specific
  clinical use, and it is not the use it's marketed for: BPC-157 for ulcerative colitis, TB-500
  and KPV for wound healing, MOTS-c for obesity and osteoporosis, **Epitalon for insomnia**. Not
  one of the seven was assessed for slowing aging.
- **Nothing has changed yet.** PCAC is advisory and non-binding; all seven remain off the list
  today; any change requires an NPRM, a 60–90 day comment period and a final rule. And it reaches
  only 503A pharmacies, not 503B outsourcing facilities.

**Two-sided block:** *Does a favourable vote mean these peptides work?*
