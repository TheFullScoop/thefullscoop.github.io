## 1. The State of AI — `ai.html`  →  Chapter 4
**"Washington narrows the door; the open models widen it"**

Rationale: Chapter 3 ended with two governments putting pre-release evaluation on the agenda.
This week that turned from evaluation into *access* — and, in the same seven days, the largest
open-weight model ever built shipped from China. That contrast is the chapter.

- **Gold Eagle launched, July 14.** A White House clearinghouse consolidating software-vulnerability
  findings across government and industry, established under the June 2 EO covered in Ch.3. Brings
  in CISA, Treasury and Defense plus unnamed open-source and critical-infrastructure partners;
  already processing reports. Nextgov flags what the announcement omits: who runs it daily, how
  sensitive data is protected, how it meshes with CISA's existing programmes.
- **The open end widened.** Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 (July 16): ~2.8T sparse MoE parameters, 1M-token
  context, largest open-weight model yet, weights promised by July 27; **#1 on Frontend Code Arena
  at 1,679**, ahead of Claude Fable 5 (1,631) and GPT-5.6 Sol (1,618); 4th on Artificial Analysis.
  A day earlier, Thinking Machines Lab's **Inkling** (975B, weights on Hugging Face) — which the
  company itself says is not the strongest model available, open or closed.
- **A missed date.** Gemini 3.5 Pro, promised for June at I/O, is months late; Bloomberg (via
  9to5Google) attributes it to coding performance after a late-June training-data reset
  disappointed. First public case of a frontier release held because the model missed its own
  maker's bar.

**Two-sided block —** *Is the government now deciding who gets the strongest models?*
CNBC (July 17, sourced to people familiar) says the White House greenlights which companies get
frontier access and will require explicit approval of partners; against that, a White House
official told CNBC it does not approve private AI releases, and the published Gold Eagle text
describes coordination with no stated power to compel. **Contested — no winner declared.**

Sources: White House, Nextgov/FCW, CNBC (×2), Tom's Hardware, Thinking Machines Lab, TechCrunch,
9to5Google, Search Engine Journal.

## 2. Göttingen — `gott.html`  →  Chapter 4
**"Eight more bombs, and a deadline to stand for office"** (German-language sources throughout)

Rationale: A direct, checkable continuation of Ch.3's bomb story, plus the civic event that will
shape the next several chapters of this book. Not contested, so no two-sided block — consistent
with Ch.2 and Ch.3.

- **The third detonation at the B27.** July 15: crews from the Gandersheim state road authority
  probing near the Leine bridge find **five more** small WWII German bombs. Prep from 11 a.m. the
  next morning, same 300 m exclusion zone, few households affected, shelter opened at the
  Nachbarschaftszentrum Holtenser Berg at 10:30. July 16: probing that morning finds **three more —
  eight in total** — all detonated on site around 1 p.m. Zone lifted, residents home, but the B27
  stayed shut pending a bridge inspection.
- **Kommunalwahl, September 13.** District assembly, city council, borough councils, plus mayor and
  district administrator. Polls 8 a.m.–6 p.m.; eligible at 16+ with German/EU citizenship and three
  months' registered main residence; runoff September 27 if needed. **Nominations were due 6 p.m.
  Monday, July 20** — the day of this run — with the electoral committee ruling July 22 in public
  session at the New Town Hall. City still recruiting poll workers.
- **The twentieth of July.** Wreath laid at 5 p.m. at the Ehrenmal on the Stauffenbergring for the
  1944 plot; two conspirators, Fritz-Dietlof Graf von der Schulenburg and Adam von Trott zu Solz,
  studied here, and the memorial stone dates to the twentieth anniversary. Closing beat: a rare
  titan arum tuber stolen from the Old Botanical Garden greenhouse between June 14 and 17, damage
  in the mid three figures.

Sources: StadtRadio Göttingen (5 separate dated articles, all verified against the article pages,
not just the index).

## 3. Money & Markets — `money.html`  →  Chapter 4
**"Inflation falls fast, and the chip trade breaks"**

Rationale: Ch.3 deliberately ended on two *scheduled* checkpoints rather than a market recap. Both
landed on July 14 and pointed opposite ways — and then the sector Ch.3 profiled fell into a bear
market. Chapter keeps Ch.3's discipline: dated, checkable fixed points, not a rolling return.

- **June CPI, July 14.** −0.4% m/m, biggest monthly drop in more than six years; annual rate 3.5%
  from May's 4.2%. Core flat on the month, 2.6% y/y. Consensus had been −0.2% and 3.8%. Energy did
  most of it, but shelter rose only 0.1% and transportation services fell 0.3%.
- **Warsh refuses the victory lap, same day.** First congressional testimony as chair: *"There might
  be some that look at this morning's data and say, 'mission accomplished.' That is not my view."*
  Five new task forces; affirmed independence; on the build-out, *"We don't know the extent to which
  the economy will benefit from the AI buildout."* September hike odds trimmed to ~63% from >75%.
- **Chips into a bear market.** S&P 500 −1.5%+ on the week (first losing week in three), Nasdaq
  −2.9%, Dow −1%. Philadelphia Semiconductor Index entered a bear market, closing Friday at
  **11,673.89** after −4%+ Thursday. Micron/SanDisk/WD −5%+, AMAT/Lam −6%+. Blamed: AI-spending ROI
  doubt, TSMC's higher capex guide, and **Kimi K3** — a direct crossover with the AI book. SK hynix,
  all of Ch.3, sells into exactly this trade. Oil back up: Brent +3% above $86, WTI +3.4% above $80
  on a sixth day of US–Iran fighting, against the ~$73 Ch.1 recorded post-ceasefire.
- **Next fixed point:** FOMC July 28–29, decision 2 p.m. ET July 29, no quarterly projections.

**Two-sided block —** *Is the chip selloff a wobble or a verdict?* Bear-market entry on AI-capex
doubt vs. 24.7% blended Q2 earnings growth with ten of eleven sectors expanding and dip-buying into
Friday's close. **Contested — no winner declared.**

Sources: BLS, CNBC (×3), CBS News, CNN, Yahoo Finance, FactSet, Federal Reserve.
