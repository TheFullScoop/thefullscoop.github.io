# Connection Wing proposal · 2026-07-18

Status: proposal for Ryan’s review; nothing here is activated or live.

## What the Parlor is today

The Parlor is a local, two-person question-card game: people select one or more decks, draw a prompt, and both answer aloud. Its house rules make passing explicit, remove scoring and time pressure, and ask that answers not be used against either person later. The page uses no accounts or trackers; deck selection and drawn-card history stay in the browser’s local storage. Sources: [`parlor.html`](../parlor.html) and the current [`about.html`](../about.html).

## 1. Chapter conversation cards

At the end of a Library chapter, offer a small printable or on-screen set of questions for two or more people: “What surprised you?”, “Which source would you open first?”, and, for contested chapters, “What is the strongest point on each side?” The prompts should point back to the chapter’s sources and never score, profile, or recommend a position.

**Why this serves a real need:** It gives people a simple way to turn solitary reading into a careful conversation about something they already chose to understand.

**How the firewall stays intact:** The prompts are finite, optional, source-bound, and viewpoint-neutral; there is no chatbot, personalization, account, notification, or engagement loop.

## 2. Read-together guides

Give selected books a one-page guide for a pair or small group: a 30-minute route through the story-so-far, one chapter, two primary sources, a matched “both sides” exchange, and a closing question about what remains uncertain. Provide print and plain-HTML versions so a host can use it at a kitchen table, classroom, or book club without giving the site anyone’s identity.

**Why this serves a real need:** It removes the work of planning a fair, bounded discussion while leaving the substance and conclusions with the people in the room.

**How the firewall stays intact:** The guide ends; it gathers no attendance, answers, contacts, or behavioral data, and every factual premise inherits the chapter’s visible sourcing.

## 3. Letter pages

Offer printable, topic-specific letter sheets that help one person write to another: “Something I learned from you,” “A question I want to understand rather than win,” or “A memory I want us to keep.” The site supplies a short structure and blank space; writing, delivery, and any reply happen entirely between people.

**Why this serves a real need:** It helps someone begin a meaningful message when the feeling is clear but the first sentence is hard.

**How the firewall stays intact:** Nothing is typed into, stored by, or sent through the site; there are no templates designed to pressure a response and no follow-up reminders.

## Suggested first build

Prototype one read-together guide for a chapter Ryan already approves. It most directly joins the two active seeds—sourced truth and human connection—while remaining a finite static page that can be reviewed line by line before any publish decision.
